LLM features inside the software you already run.

What it is

A summarize button on a record, automatic classification of incoming tickets, field extraction from uploaded documents, a drafting assistant in a text box, a chat panel inside a product that already exists. GTDataWorks adds the feature to your web app, CRM or data pipeline through the OpenAI, Anthropic or Cohere APIs, in FastAPI, Flask or Node.js, behind the authentication you already have, with no re-platforming.

Your users see one more control in software they already know, and it behaves like the rest of the product: it answers in a predictable time, fails with a clear message rather than a hang, and does not surprise you on the invoice. That comes from the part of the scope that is not the model: rate limiting, per-user and per-month spend caps, a circuit breaker for when the provider is slow, and a log of every call so you can see cost and usage by feature.

How the work runs

The build steps once the quote is agreed. The brief, scoping call and quote are the same for every service.

  1. Surface and contract

    Which screens or endpoints get the feature, what goes into each model call and what comes back, and which authentication it sits behind. Written as an API contract before any code.

  2. Thin vertical slice

    One endpoint end to end against the real provider, with tests and a stub for your CI, so the shape is proven in the first days rather than the last.

  3. Production guards

    Rate limits, spend caps, timeouts and a circuit breaker, plus structured logging of every call with its cost. Built before the feature widens, not after the first bill.

  4. Full build and documentation

    The remaining endpoints or UI hooks, the API documentation, and the cost and usage dashboard.

  5. Staged rollout

    A rollout plan that starts behind a flag or with a small user group, watches cost and error rate, and widens on the numbers rather than on a date.

Where it fits

A good fit when

  • The product exists and works; the model adds one capability to it.
  • You can describe the feature as input in, output out, with a known place in the UI or the pipeline.
  • Predictable cost and clean authentication matter more than novelty.

Not a fit when

  • The feature has to answer from your documents and show its sources. That is retrieval work: Conversational AI & RAG Agents.
  • It is a multi-step process across several systems with decisions in the middle. That is Agent Orchestration & Workflow Automation.
  • You want Claude or ChatGPT to reach into your data and tools, rather than your product calling a model. That is Custom LLM Connectors & MCP Tools.

Questions

Which provider should the feature use?

The one that fits the task and the agreements you already have: OpenAI, Anthropic or Cohere through the API, on your own keys so spend is billed to you. The integration is written behind a thin adapter, so the provider can change later without touching the product code around it.

Does any of my data leave my systems?

Only the text sent to the model provider for each call, under your agreement with them, and only the fields the contract names. Logs stay in your infrastructure. GTDataWorks does not train on your data and keeps no copy of it after handover.

How do I know the output is right?

Every integration ships with tests: schema checks on what the model returns, fixtures for the failure modes (timeouts, refusals, malformed output) and a small set of real inputs with expected results. Where the content itself has to be right, as in classification or extraction, a graded test set like the one in Prompt Engineering & Fine-Tuning is added to the scope.

What happens after handover?

You own the code, the tests, the documentation and the dashboard, and the rollout plan is yours to run. Later changes are scoped as new work. If the feature grows into a full assistant over your documents, Conversational AI & RAG Agents is the next step.
